# Continuity Atlas research — 2026-10-17

## Selected hypothesis

During a multi-region outage, a controller that combines incident-analogue recall,
Wasserstein distributionally robust CVaR ranking, Rego authorization, and sealed
decision evidence will preserve tier-0 services more reliably than any ingredient-only
baseline. Each ingredient must change an observed capability in a fresh execution.

## Candidate comparison

- **Continuity Atlas — selected:** a regional failover control system spanning topology,
  uncertainty, memory, policy, evidence, and recovery.
- **Rego plan linter — rejected:** a narrow validator without persistence,
  orchestration, recovery, or end-to-end evaluation.
- **Incident similarity CLI — rejected:** a RuVector wrapper without policy or a
  continuity-control workload.
- **Retry-budget service — rejected:** materially overlaps JitterMap.

This capability is distinct from Provenance Firebreak's artifact-quarantine problem,
ToolRecall's memory evaluation, and JitterMap's retry-admission control.

## Frozen evaluation design

Seven fresh executions use the same topology, correlated-region scenarios, and seed:
combined; without RuVector; without RVF; without Regorus; without DRO;
enterprise-only; and Ruvnet-only. Capability flags and objective bounds are compared,
not inferred from dependency presence.
